# Physics-informed-learning-captures-nonmonotonic-pore-scale-biofilm-growth
This code is from the paper 'Physics-Informed Learning Captures Nonmonotonic Pore-Scale Biofilm Growth,' with Qiang Ye as the first author.

Our research mainly covers the following points:
Accurately modeling biofilm growth in porous media is essential for environmental remediation, medical treatment and energy development. However, simulating this process remains challenging due to the intricate interaction between physical and biochemical dynamics. Our study reveals that biofilm growth and diffusion exhibit complex non-monotonic relationships with biomass, modulated by environmental and intrinsic biological factors. Based on a modified density-dependent biofilm diffusion model, a hybrid framework for biofilm growth simulation combining microfluidic experiments with a physics-informed neural network (PINN) was developed. Compared to numerical simulations and purely data-driven methods, the hybrid model offers more accurate simulations of biofilm growth and diffusion under experimental conditions (with a coefficient of determination of 93%) and successfully inverts the optimal correction parameters for the mechanisms. Utilizing transfer learning, the high generalization capability of PINN was validated in a new dynamic scenario with sparse data. This high-fidelity framework provides a powerful tool for biofilm growth modeling and offers new theoretical insights and strategies for related applications.
